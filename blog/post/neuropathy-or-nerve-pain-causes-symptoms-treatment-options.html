Neuropathy or nerve pain: Causes, symptoms & treatment options

What is neuropathy?

Neuropathy, or peripheral neuropathy, occurs when the peripheral nerves (those outside the brain and spinal cord) are damaged, disrupted, or destroyed. These nerves are responsible for carrying signals between your brain and the rest of your body, controlling sensation, movement, and automatic functions like heart rate and digestion.

When these nerves malfunction, the body sends confused or distorted signals that the brain interprets as pain, tingling, numbness, or weakness. Neuropathy can affect a single nerve (mononeuropathy) or multiple nerve groups simultaneously (polyneuropathy), making its presentation highly individual.

Common causes of nerve pain

The root causes of neuropathy are wide-ranging, and identifying the underlying trigger is essential for selecting the right treatment approach. Several medical conditions, lifestyle factors, and external agents can damage peripheral nerves over time.

  • Diabetes: Diabetic neuropathy is by far the most prevalent form, resulting from prolonged high blood sugar damaging nerve fibers throughout the body, most commonly in the legs and feet.
  • Vitamin deficiencies: Deficiencies in B1, B6, B12, and E vitamins can impair nerve function and lead to progressive neuropathic symptoms.
  • Autoimmune diseases: Conditions such as lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and Guillain-Barré syndrome cause the immune system to mistakenly attack nerve tissue.
  • Infections: Viral and bacterial infections, including shingles (herpes zoster), Lyme disease, and HIV, can directly inflame or damage peripheral nerves.
  • Alcohol abuse: Excessive alcohol consumption is toxic to nerve fibers and is one of the leading preventable causes of neuropathy in the UK.
  • Medications and chemotherapy: Certain prescription drugs, particularly chemotherapy agents, can cause treatment-induced neuropathy as a side effect.
  • Physical trauma or compression: Injuries, repetitive strain, or conditions such as carpal tunnel syndrome can compress and damage individual nerves.
  • Hereditary conditions: Genetic disorders like Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease cause inherited forms of neuropathy that develop progressively from birth or early life.

Recognising the symptoms of neuropathy or nerve pain

Neuropathy symptoms can vary considerably depending on which nerve type is affected — sensory, motor, or autonomic — and where in the body the damage has occurred. Early recognition of these warning signs is critical to preventing further nerve deterioration.

  • Burning or shooting pain: A persistent, intense burning sensation, often described as "pins and needles" or electric shocks, particularly in the hands and feet.
  • Numbness and tingling: Gradual loss of sensation in the extremities, which may spread upwards towards the limbs over time.
  • Muscle weakness: Motor nerve damage can cause noticeable muscle weakness, cramping, or difficulty with coordination and balance.
  • Extreme sensitivity to touch: Even light contact, such as bedsheets resting on skin, can trigger disproportionate pain (allodynia).
  • Loss of reflexes: Diminished or absent tendon reflexes, particularly in the ankles or knees, are a common clinical sign.
  • Autonomic symptoms: When autonomic nerves are affected, symptoms include blood pressure fluctuations, excessive sweating, digestive problems, and bladder dysfunction.
  • Sleep disturbances: Nighttime pain intensification is common, making it difficult to sleep and further impacting overall health and mental well-being.

Effective treatment options for nerve pain

Managing neuropathy requires a tailored, multi-pronged approach that addresses both the underlying cause and the pain itself. Treatment ranges from lifestyle adjustments to prescription medications designed to calm overactive nerve signals.

  • Treating the underlying cause: Managing diabetes with blood sugar control, correcting vitamin deficiencies, or addressing autoimmune conditions can slow or even reverse neuropathic progression.
  • Prescription nerve pain medications: Medicines such as pregabalin (Lyrica) and gabapentin are widely prescribed in the UK for neuropathic pain. They work by stabilizing overactive electrical signals in damaged nerves, providing significant relief.
  • Tricyclic antidepressants and SNRIs: Drugs like amitriptyline and duloxetine are frequently used off-label for nerve pain management due to their effectiveness in modulating pain pathways.
  • Topical treatments: Lidocaine patches and capsaicin cream applied directly to the affected area can provide localized relief without systemic side effects.
  • Physical therapy: Targeted physiotherapy can strengthen muscles weakened by motor neuropathy, improve balance, and reduce injury risk.
  • Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (TENS): TENS devices deliver low-voltage electrical currents to interrupt pain signals traveling to the brain, offering drug-free relief.
  • Lifestyle modifications: Reducing alcohol intake, following a nerve-healthy diet rich in B vitamins, maintaining a healthy weight, and quitting smoking all support long-term nerve health.
  • Pain management clinics: For complex or chronic neuropathy, referral to a specialist pain clinic may offer advanced therapies, including nerve blocks or spinal cord stimulation.

Frequently Asked Questions About Neuropathy

What is the most common type of neuropathy?

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy is the most common form, affecting up to 50% of people living with diabetes. It primarily causes pain, numbness, and tingling in the feet and legs due to prolonged elevated blood sugar levels damaging nerve fibers.

Can neuropathy be cured completely?

Whether neuropathy can be cured depends entirely on its underlying cause. If caught early and the root trigger — such as a vitamin deficiency or poorly controlled diabetes — is addressed effectively, significant improvement or even full recovery is possible. Chronic cases are more commonly managed than cured.

What does neuropathic pain feel like?

Neuropathic pain is often described as burning, stabbing, shooting, or electric shock-like sensations. Some people experience hypersensitivity where even gentle touch causes intense pain, while others feel persistent numbness or a "dead" feeling in the affected area.

What medications are best for nerve pain in the UK?

Pregabalin, gabapentin, amitriptyline, and duloxetine are among the most commonly prescribed medications for neuropathic pain in the UK. The most suitable option depends on your specific symptoms, medical history, and any other medications you are taking.

Is neuropathy a sign of something serious?

Neuropathy itself is a serious condition requiring proper medical attention, but it can also indicate an underlying health issue such as diabetes, an autoimmune disorder, or nutritional deficiencies. Any persistent numbness, burning, or unexplained pain in the extremities warrants a consultation with your GP.

Does neuropathy get worse over time?

Without treatment, neuropathy can progress and worsen significantly. Identifying and treating the cause early, alongside appropriate pain management, is key to slowing progression and maintaining quality of life.

Can lifestyle changes improve neuropathy symptoms?

Yes, lifestyle changes can make a meaningful difference. Reducing alcohol consumption, controlling blood sugar, eating a balanced diet rich in B vitamins, quitting smoking, and engaging in regular low-impact exercise have all been shown to ease neuropathic symptoms and support nerve health over time.

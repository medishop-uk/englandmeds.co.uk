Side effect of Ativan 2 mg

Ativan 2 mg may cause side effects, especially when starting treatment, increasing the dose, or using it with alcohol or other sedating medicines. Patients should follow the prescribed dose carefully and contact a healthcare professional if side effects feel severe, unusual, or worrying.

  • Drowsiness: Feeling sleepy or very tired in the daytime is a common side effect of lorazepam.
  • Dizziness: Some patients may feel light-headed, unsteady, or slower than usual.
  • Muscle weakness: Lorazepam may cause weakness or reduced coordination in some people.
  • Reduced alertness: It may affect concentration, judgement, reaction time, and the ability to drive safely.
  • Dependence risk: Regular or incorrect use may lead to tolerance, dependence, and withdrawal symptoms when stopped suddenly.

FAQ about lorazepam

What is lorazepam used for?

Lorazepam is a prescription benzodiazepine used for anxiety-related symptoms and sleeping problems linked with anxiety when a doctor decides it is suitable. It may also be used in specific clinical settings for sedation or seizure-related care, but patients should only use it for the reason stated by their prescriber.

Is Ativan the same as lorazepam?

Ativan is a well-known brand name for lorazepam. The active ingredient is lorazepam, but packaging, manufacturer, availability, and pricing may vary between branded Ativan and generic lorazepam tablets.

Is lorazepam 2 mg strong?

Lorazepam 2 mg is a higher strength and should be taken only as prescribed. Patients should not increase the dose, repeat doses early, or use someone else’s medicine because this can increase the risk of heavy sedation, confusion, breathing problems, and dependence.

How long does lorazepam take to work?

Lorazepam tablets usually begin to work within about 20 to 30 minutes, and the sedating effect may last for around 6 to 8 hours. The exact effect can vary depending on dose, age, health condition, and other medicines.

Can I drink alcohol with lorazepam?

Alcohol should be avoided while taking lorazepam because it can increase sleepiness, dizziness, poor coordination, and breathing-related risk. Mixing alcohol with benzodiazepines can be dangerous, especially if other sedating medicines are also involved.

Can I stop taking lorazepam suddenly?

You should not stop lorazepam suddenly if you have been taking it regularly. Sudden stopping may cause withdrawal symptoms such as anxiety rebound, sleep problems, irritability, sweating, shaking, or more serious effects. Always speak with a doctor before reducing or stopping treatment.
